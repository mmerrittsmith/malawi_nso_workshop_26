# Lecture 3 figures — what each one is for

Rebuild them all with `python3 prep/scripts/make_lecture3_figures.py`. Every figure is made from
data the participants have already opened, so nothing in the lecture is a stock picture.

Every number below was measured from the files on the drive, not quoted from a paper.

---

### `01_directly_observable_flood.png` — *directly observable*

Nsanje, 14 March 2025. Left: the color image. Right: their own Day 1 result, water in blue and the
buildings standing in it in red.

**The line:** this is the easy case. The thing we wanted to count was physically visible, and we
counted it. Hold on to this feeling, because the rest of the lecture takes it away.

### `02_threshold_decides_the_answer.png` — *the number depends on a decision*

Chikwawa, the same image three times, water in magenta, with the cut-off moved from 9,000 to 10,000
to 11,000. **12, 55 and 156 flooded buildings.** Thirteen times the answer, one image, three
defensible choices.

**The line:** nobody here is wrong. The number is not a property of the flood, it is a property of
the flood *and a decision somebody made*. So report the decision alongside the number.

### `03_dry_soil_trap.png` — *the obvious method fails*

Zomba in July, dry season. Middle: a built-up index (SWIR against NIR) calls **82%** of the square
built-up. Right: the built-up layer says **3%**. Dry bare soil reflects almost exactly like a roof.

**The line:** the obvious method was not a little bit wrong, it was 27 times wrong, and it looked
completely convincing. The check that caught it was asking whether the answer was plausible, not
anything clever about satellites.

### `04_likoma_lights_mislead.png` — *a signal appearing is not the thing appearing*

Night lights summed in the 3 km circle, every year 2012–2022. Likoma is dark for nine years and
lights up in **2021** — during the worst tourism years there have been.

**The line:** if you had this chart and nothing else, you would write "tourism boom on Likoma" and
it would be published. The lights are real; the interpretation is invented. **We have not confirmed
why they appeared** — do not offer a cause from the front of the room, ask the room for candidates
(a power supply change is the obvious guess).

Worth adding: the product sets faint background light to zero, so somewhere that gets slightly
brighter can jump from "0" to "lit" in a single year rather than rising gradually. Part of the
switch is the measurement, not the island.

### `05_no_signal_is_not_no_activity.png` — *absence of evidence*

Nkhotakota (Tongole): one lodge, about 20 people, no lights, no mapped buildings, 0.6 ha built-up.
Cape Maclear: 25 lodges, 2,905 mapped buildings, lit in both years.

**The line:** both are tourism. One is invisible to every layer we have. A map of what satellites
can see is not a map of what is happening, and the places that disappear are systematically the
small, rural, low-impact ones. For a statistics office, that bias is the finding.
